## 12. Is the conclusion overstated? OVERSTATED

My verdict on the published narrative is that the DECISION is correct and better supported
than the doc claims, and the ATTRIBUTION is not supported at anything like the stated
strength.

**First, the statistics on the run as published.** Exact two-sided sign tests on the paired
splits, plus 200,000-sample bootstrap intervals on the mean per-scenario delta:

```
Comparison        W   L   T   meanDelta(pts)   95% bootstrap CI      exact sign-test p
bplus vs b       22   7  31     3.10           [ 1.07,  5.00]        p = 0.0081
d vs bplus        7  15  38    -1.31           [-2.86,  0.48]        p = 0.1338
d vs b           20  11  29     1.79           [ 0.24,  3.33]        p = 0.1496
b vs a           53   3   4    20.12           [15.95, 24.52]        p = 0.0000
```

So on the face of it: B+ over B clears a Bonferroni-corrected 0.0125 threshold for four
comparisons, and D versus B+ does NOT. The D versus B+ interval spans zero. The honest
reading of the published run alone is "no detectable gain from the reference, and a gain as
large as the 6-point decision margin is ruled out", not "the reference contributed nothing".
Those are different claims and the doc asserts the stronger one. The whole D versus B+ story
is a swing of 5.5 rubric points out of 420, with 38 of 60 scenarios exact ties.

**Second, and this is the finding that decides my answer.** The six batches were graded by
six independent graders, ten scenarios each, all four arms of a scenario graded by the same
grader. I computed each arm's mean by batch:

Batch 6 is not a strictness outlier. Its arm A sits at 64.3, right inside the 60.7 to 65.0

the longest sheet every time. The two splits are not two findings, they are one batch seen

produces a large positive B+ vs B and a large negative D vs B+ simultaneously.

Where the conclusion is FAIR, and I want this recorded as clearly as the criticism:

- The SHIP decision is correct and my analysis strengthens it. The pre-committed rule
  returned INCONCLUSIVE at a 2-point D-over-B gap, and nothing shipped. On my numbers

Where it is OVERSTATED:

- "**The procedure is the effect**" is the least supported claim in the document and it is
  the one carried forward as the finding worth keeping. Line 195: "That is worth +3 points
  and 22 scenarios to 7 over free-form documentation use, and it needs none of this reference

  the failure mode that actually occurred and which the paired design does not protect

**Would the README mislead a reader who did not open the results file? Yes, in two specific
ways.** README lines 98 to 103 read:

## What the spec missed

Things I checked that the spec did not ask for, and defects I consider real regardless of
whether a check covers them.

**1. The single-batch dependency of the entire conclusion. This is the headline defect.**
The spec's check 5 asks whether the paired splits match, and they do, and check 12 asks
whether they support the conclusion. Neither asks for a stability analysis, and the run has
a natural six-fold split by grader that makes one trivial. Both published directions are one
grader on ten scenarios. Full evidence in check 12. If one thing changes as a result of this
review it should be this: `tests/results-tier3.md` needs a per-batch table and a
leave-one-batch-out row, and the "What this says" section needs rewriting around
"50 scenarios and five graders found no difference among the three docs arms on any field".

**2. Verbosity is an uncontrolled variable and it tracks the result.** Arm B+ wrote the
longest sheet in 26 of 60 scenarios against a chance expectation of 15, and in 10 of 10 in
the batch that carries the result. Nothing in the prompts caps or normalizes answer length,
and the rubric's 0.5 tier ("materially incomplete") rewards length directly. This is also a
partial de-anonymization channel: in batch 6, "the longest sheet" was a perfect predictor of
arm B+.

**3. The citation rate at 100/100 is a saturated detector that proves less than it appears
to.** The scorer's test is `typeof c === 'string' && /^https?:\/\//.test(c.trim())`. It
checks that a string looks like a URL. It does not check that the URL was fetched, that it
resolves, or that it supports the field. Cross-referencing the citations against the four
pages the doc says returned unusable dumps:

```
bplus  citations=240  pointing at a page the doc says returned an unusable dump = 58 (24%)
d      citations=240  pointing at a page the doc says returned an unusable dump = 47 (20%)
```

So roughly a fifth to a quarter of the citations that produce the "100%" figure point at
pages the same document says could not be read. The results file presents citation rate as
"the detector for the premortem's likeliest failure: an arm that stops fetching and answers
from memory". At 100% for both arms the detector fired for nobody and has no discriminating
power; it measured format compliance, not verification.

**4. The batch design's real strength is undersold, and its real weakness is undisclosed.**
Grading all four arms of a scenario with one grader means batch-level strictness cancels in
the paired comparison, which is genuinely good design and is why the paired analysis is the
right one. But it also means a single grader's differential treatment of the arms enters the
result undamped, at one sixth weight, and nothing in the harness detects that. The scorer
gates hard on completeness and not at all on between-batch consistency. A per-batch
dispersion check would have caught this before publication.

**5. The B+ vs B paired row was added to the reported output in the same commit as the
data.** See check 2. The function was pre-committed at 174fb6d; the decision to report that
specific pair was made at 60ee298 alongside the answers. It is the row the headline leans on
hardest.

**6. `key-defects.jsonl` has no schema and no gate.** Its `scenario` field accepts free text
and two of 27 records use it for a scenario list and a range, which is what produces the
"26 of 60" undercount in check 10. Every other Tier 3 artifact is gated; this one is not.

**7. A grader violated an explicit grading instruction and said so in the record.** Record
15, batch 4: "I graded these 0.5 throughout rather than 0", against `grader.md` line 41
"Do not soften a score to compensate for a key you dislike." I verified the effect is
uniform across the docs arms, so it does not bias the comparison, but the results file's
claim that graders were "required to grade to the key anyway and record the disagreement
rather than adjust a score" describes the instruction, not what happened.

**8. `arm-a.md` tells the arm A agent the expected answer.** Its framing paragraph names the
pilot range and the historical 71% target. Inside an allowed diff class, but it means the
"70% against 71% is a near-exact replication" argument is not as independent as it reads.

**9. Position balance is fine globally, uneven in batch 6.** Across 60 scenarios all 24
sheet orderings occur and arm A sits in each of the four positions exactly 15 times. Within
batch 6, B+ never appears in sheet position 1 (0 of 10) and appears in positions 2 and 4
four times each. That is a chance consequence of a deterministic per-id permutation, not a
design flaw, and I do not think a grader could have used it, but it is worth knowing that
the balance guarantee is global rather than per batch.
